| 2026-10-09 | harbor-omp | The adapter standard's largest gap — no ATIF trajectory (`capabilities.atif` false, `convert_trajectory` returning `None`) — was implemented losslessly rather than in the nearest peer's shape, and the constraints that decided the design only appeared while doing it | Three ATIF rules pushed back on a one-step-per-session-line encoding: `source` is one of `system`/`user`/`agent`, so a `toolResult` line has no idiomatic home; a non-agent step may not carry `metrics`, so the auxiliary model's `model_usage` records cannot be step metrics; and an observation result may only name a tool call of *its own* step, so folding a result into the calling step would leave that line unaccounted for. The two moves that follow — a `toolResult` as its own system step naming its call in `extra.omp_tool_call_id`, and `final_metrics` taken from the session reader instead of re-summed from the steps — are what keep the artifact lossless *and* equal to `AgentContext` | Measure the losslessness instead of asserting it: one step per non-blank line, the line's decoded payload verbatim under `extra.omp_event`, and a loud `TrajectoryAccountingError` when a line has no step; gate the write with Harbor's own validator so a document a consumer would reject never lands | Shipped `harbor_omp/trajectory.py` (the one converter both paths use, plus the validated atomic write), the accounting view `session.read_session_lines`, `capabilities.atif = True`, the post-run writer in `omp_agent.py`, and five VERIFY rows whose red cases were each exercised — dropping an event, un-gating the validator, re-summing the steps, dropping undecodable lines, dropping the post-run write, flipping the flag | `src/harbor_omp/trajectory.py`; `src/harbor_omp/session.py`; `src/harbor_omp/omp_agent.py`; `tests/test_trajectory.py`; VERIFY.md; README.md; docs/architecture.md |
